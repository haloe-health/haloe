What Hijama Is

What is hijama?

Hijama — wet cupping — is one of the oldest traditions in natural wellbeing, practised for thousands of years and encouraged by the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. Cups are placed on the skin to create gentle suction, and a small number of light, superficial incisions are made before a second, shorter round of suction. Clients most often describe the sensation as a firm pulling tug, and the incisions themselves as a quick, light scratch. Sessions are generally found relaxing, and many people feel lighter and more settled afterwards. haloe also offers dry cupping — suction only, with no incisions — and massage, for anyone who'd rather ease in gently or prefers not to have their skin broken.

haloe offers complementary wellness therapy to support relaxation, circulation and general wellbeing. It is not a substitute for medical advice, diagnosis or treatment.

Safety & Hygiene

Is hijama safe?

Yes — every session follows strict hygiene practice, with single-use, sterile equipment for each client. Halima is CMA and IPHM accredited and fully insured, and every booking includes a short health and consent form beforehand so any medical considerations are known in advance. Circular marks are common after cupping and usually fade within 3 to 10 days — this is a normal, expected response, not a sign that anything has gone wrong. As with any wellness therapy, hijama isn't right for everyone; the health form helps identify anything that needs a closer look before your appointment.

Timing

Hijama on sunnah days

Hijama is traditionally sought on the 17th, 19th or 21st of the Islamic lunar month, and Mondays, Tuesdays or Thursdays are also commonly preferred. Where it's possible to align your appointment with one of these days, Halima is glad to help you plan around it — though availability on the day still comes first, so it's worth asking early. The Tuesday clinic at Milton Hall, Deansgate naturally falls on one of the preferred weekdays every week, which is part of why it's structured that way. Mobile visits can be booked on Sunnah dates too, subject to availability across Halima's diary that week.

The majority scholarly view is that hijama does not break a fast, though opinions differ — sessions can be scheduled around Ramadan and your energy levels.
