What Hijama Is

A traditional practice, gently applied

Hijama — wet cupping — is one of the oldest traditions in natural wellbeing, practised for thousands of years and encouraged by the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. Cups are placed on the skin to create gentle suction, and a small number of light, superficial incisions are made before a second, shorter round of suction.

Clients most often describe the sensation as a firm pulling tug, and the incisions themselves as a quick, light scratch. Sessions are generally found relaxing, and many people feel lighter and more settled afterwards. haloe also offers dry cupping (suction only, no incisions) and massage, for those who'd rather ease into the experience or prefer not to have their skin broken.

haloe offers complementary wellness therapy to support relaxation, circulation and general wellbeing. It is not a substitute for medical advice, diagnosis or treatment.

Timing

The Sunnah days

Hijama is traditionally sought on the 17th, 19th or 21st of the Islamic lunar month, and Mondays, Tuesdays or Thursdays are also commonly preferred. Where it's possible to align your appointment with one of these days, Halima is glad to help you plan around it — though availability on the day still comes first, so it's worth asking early.

FAQ

A few quick answers

Does hijama hurt?

The suction feels like a firm tug. The small incisions used in wet cupping feel like a quick, light scratch. Most clients describe the session itself as relaxing — some fall asleep.

Will I need to undress?

Only the area being treated is uncovered. You stay draped throughout and your modesty is fully respected.

What are the marks from cupping?

Circular marks are common after cupping and usually fade within 3 to 10 days. This is a normal, expected response, not a sign that anything has gone wrong.

Can I have hijama while fasting in Ramadan?

The majority scholarly view is that it does not break the fast, though opinions differ. Halima is happy to schedule around Ramadan and your energy levels — just ask.

How do I prepare for my appointment?

Eat a light meal beforehand, wear something comfortable you can loosen, and avoid alcohol and blood-thinning medication where possible. A short health form is sent before every session.

What's the difference between the clinic day and a mobile visit?

The clinic day is every Tuesday at Milton Hall, Deansgate — a private treatment room, paid in full at booking, no travel fee. A mobile visit is Halima coming to you anywhere across Greater Manchester, on any other day, with the usual travel note and deposit-free full payment at checkout.
